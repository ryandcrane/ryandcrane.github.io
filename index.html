PROJECT 01 · BIOPROTEAN LAB, ASU · JAN 2025 – PRESENT · SOLE ENGINEER

Vision-Guided Organoid Culture Automation Workcell

A microscope, moving stage, pipetting robot, and robot arm that feed and image living human organoids without the pipette ever touching them.

THE PROBLEM

The lab grows cortical organoids, human mini-brains made from stem cells, to study how microglia shape brain development and injury response. Keeping them alive means feeding and imaging every plate by hand, every day.

WHAT I BUILT

As the only engineer, I built the well-scanning stage from a repurposed laser engraver. It carries a plate of organoids over a stationary brightfield microscope, all running inside an automated pipetting robot, with a robot arm outside swapping plates with the incubator.

PROJECT 02 · BIOPROTEAN LAB, ASU · SUBSYSTEM OF PROJECT 01 · DESIGN, BUILD, FIRMWARE

Motorized Focusing Stage and Well-Scanning Microscope

The optics inside the workcell. Its own subsystem, with its own failures.

WHAT I BUILT

A NEMA stepper drives a lead screw that moves a carriage along a linear guide rail, translating the 10X objective toward or away from the sample. A GRBL G-code routine runs autofocus while the scanning microscope steps through each well.

The optics support two modes in one box. A white LED lights the sample for brightfield; for widefield fluorescence, a laser passes through collimating and condensing lenses to a dichroic mirror. Both paths return through a tube lens to a color CMOS camera.

WHAT FOUGHT BACK

The Z axis fought back three ways: drift, backlash, and friction. Backlash went away once I keyed the motor shaft to the lead screw more robustly. Drift and friction came down after I redesigned and reprinted the casing to hold motor, screw, and rail in tighter alignment.

PROJECT 03 · iGEM TEAM, ASU · MAR – NOV 2024 · HARDWARE ENGINEER · PUBLISHED 2026

The OptoPACE Bioreactor

A turbidostat that evolves proteins on demand, with light as the selection knob. The hardware behind my first-author paper.

HOW IT WORKS

A laser and photoresistor measure bacterial concentration in the first vessel, holding E. coli at mid-log growth by pumping fresh media in as old culture flows out.

That culture flows into a second vessel of bacteriophages, which carry custom plasmids into the bacteria. The plasmids build a protein of our choosing under selection pressure, so bacteria making better proteins survive and pass the DNA on while the rest die out.

Light sets that selection pressure. Purple activates the promoter on the custom plasmid, green suppresses it, so the survival threshold is tunable mid-run.

BUILT FROM OFF-THE-SHELF PARTS

DC stir motors, one peristaltic pump, an LCD, a rotary encoder, an LED strip, and a laser-photoresistor pair. Every housing part is 3D printed.

PROJECT 04 · iGEM TEAM, ASU · 2025 · HARDWARE TEAM LEAD

Portable Electrochemical Sensor Enclosure

A field enclosure for a blood-typing sensor in our team's enzymatic blood-conversion kit for clinical emergencies.

HOW IT WORKS

Lectins bind carbohydrate sugars. Each one is tied to a nitric-oxide-generating molecule that undergoes redox reactions, so when the complex latches onto a blood type it produces an electrochemical readout of antigen concentration by cyclic voltammetry.

WHAT I BUILT

Prototype 1 was breadboards around an Arduino-compatible board. Prototype 2 moved to a compact ESP32 potentiostat and a screen-printed electrode in a printed enclosure sized for the kit. I led weekly hardware meetings and designed the casing.

BEYOND THE BENCH

I interviewed stakeholders at W. L. Gore and wrote the business plan for taking the device to market and pursuing a patent. The team continuing the project still works from it.

PROJECT 05 · INDEPENDENT · JAN – MAY 2026 · FEniCS

Finite Element Modeling of Intestinal Organoids

WHY IT MATTERS

An organoid's internal mechanics shape what it becomes. But there is no way to measure them without destroying the organoid, which is exactly what makes a finite element model worth building.

WHAT I DID

I independently replicated a published finite element model of intestinal organoids (bioRxiv, 2022), porting it from Abaqus to open-source FEniCS: meshes from uniform (Fibonacci) and random seed points, a Voronoi shell with apical and basal surfaces, and von Mises stress compared across three maturity stages.

PROJECT 06 · NSF ATHENA AI INSTITUTE, DUKE · 2026 · RESEARCH ASSISTANT

Memristive Synapse for Silicon Neurons

HOW IT WORKS

A CMOS circuit of transistors and capacitors that reproduces cortical spiking: membrane voltage accumulation, threshold and reset, and excitatory or inhibitory synapses. The synaptic weight lives in the memristor itself, so a spiking network can run on the chip with no external memory.

RESULT

Eight distinct cortical firing patterns from one circuit. Sweeping memristance from 1 to 100 megaohms drops inter-spike interval from 76.82 ms to 9.39 ms and stretches intraburst time from 2.06 ms to 9.25 ms. One analog weight tunes both spike timing and burst structure.
